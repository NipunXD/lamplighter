# Form text (Razorpay AI Builder Internship 2026 — Track 3: AI Revenue Recovery)

## Project Name / Title
Lamplighter — a bounded AI revenue-recovery agent for Razorpay merchants

## Project Objectives (What does it solve?)
Merchants lose revenue in small, undramatic ways: a UPI collect request times out, a card hits its daily limit, a bank is briefly down, an AutoPay mandate is paused, a B2B invoice drifts past due. Each one is recoverable, but chasing them by hand is slow and chasing them with a dumb cron annoys customers and breaks rules. Lamplighter is an agent that works a batch of at-risk revenue end to end: it diagnoses the root cause from Razorpay's error objects and raw bank strings (rules first, a local LLM for the long tail), lets a policy engine enumerate and gate every possible intervention (quiet hours 21:00–09:00 IST, DND registry, three-touch cap, no repeats, no retries on hard declines, goodwill-aware expected value, incentive budget, escalation only with a stated reason), lets the model choose among the approved options and write the customer's message in their language (Hinglish or English, validated for compliance), executes through real Razorpay test-mode Orders and Standard Checkout with signature verification and capture, and reports measured money recovered against a naive-retry baseline with an honest exception list. Everything the agent does lands in a hash-chained audit log. The UI renders the batch as a cozy dusk-time town — every customer a house, every at-risk payment a dark lantern, the agent a lamplighter who rests at night — so that reasoning, money actions and stopping rules are legible at a glance rather than buried in a table, and a week-in-review layer turns the batch into charts: recovery over time against the baseline with the quiet-hour bands visible, where the money went from case kind to root cause to outcome, and what the model changed.

## Build Challenges & Technical Obstacles
1. Razorpay test mode caps Payment Links at 30 per account (a 429 with "test mode limit of 30 reached"), and cancelling links does not free the quota. I hit it in the first real batch. I replaced Payment Links with Orders plus a self-hosted Standard Checkout page, which has no cap and is a more complete integration: HMAC-SHA256 signature verification, capture of authorized payments, and polling of order payments so a lantern relights even if the browser handler never fires. 2. Test-mode bursts also return plain 429s; a naive retry made it worse. The client now serialises calls, spaces them ~700 ms apart, backs off 2/4/8 s on 429, and opens a circuit after three failures that the engine waits out instead of failing a whole simulated week in twenty wall-clock seconds. 3. A 4B local model dropped the merchant name or ignored the Hinglish instruction in about half of its drafts. Rather than trust it, I put a validator between the model and the customer (exact amount, merchant, link placeholder, opt-out line, banned threatening words, Roman script only) with template fallback, then fixed the prompt with a style example and a verbatim "must contain" list; acceptance went from ~50% to ~100% and every rejection is still logged. 4. Making "money recovered" honest without real customers: a seeded simulator with hidden state the agent never reads (archetype, salary day, annoyance threshold, willingness to say STOP), the same world for the agent and the baseline, payments after the window not counted, and a report that says exactly which numbers are simulated and which are verified on Razorpay. 5. The first version of the policy let the agent send the same email three times to a silent customer and treated a UPI collect retry as "silent"; a no-repeat rule, a goodwill cost in expected value, and moving UPI retries into customer-facing touches cut complaints by half in the batch.
